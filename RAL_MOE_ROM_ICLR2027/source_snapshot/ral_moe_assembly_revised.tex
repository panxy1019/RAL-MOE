\section{Admissibility-Constrained Hierarchical Assembly}
\label{sec:hierarchical_ral_moe}

The regime-local specialists define independent reduced dynamical models and
therefore require an outer mechanism to determine which local descriptions
are relevant at a given parameter value and how neighboring predictions
should be combined.  RAL-MoE-ROM separates this task into three elements:
regime preference, adjacency of the local models, and dynamical
admissibility.  In regime interiors a single specialist is used, whereas in
an overlap two adjacent specialists may be advanced independently and
combined after reconstruction in the common physical space.

\subsection{Regime routing and adjacency constraints}
\label{subsec:trajectory_router_topology}

For the scalar parameter considered here, the outer E2 router receives the
standardized input
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\mu}
  =
  \frac{\mu-\mu_{R}}{\sigma_{R}},
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol{\pi}(\mu)
  =
  \operatorname{softmax}
  \bigl(\mathcal{R}_{E2}(\widetilde{\mu})\bigr)
  =
  [\pi_S,\pi_H,\pi_P]^{\mathsf T}.
  \label{eq:e2_outer_router}
\end{equation}
For a fixed-$\mu$ trajectory, the routing probabilities are evaluated once
and remain unchanged during the subsequent rollout.  The router therefore
provides a parameter-dependent preference among regime-local models rather
than a time-dependent switching law.

The admissible adjacency of the three regimes is represented by
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{adj}}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    1&1&0\\
    1&1&1\\
    0&1&1
  \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:adjacency_matrix}
\end{equation}
with class ordering $(S,H,P)$.  If $k$ is the regime preferred by the router,
the topologically admissible candidate set is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{top}}(k)
  =
  \{j\in\mathcal{R}:A_{\mathrm{adj},kj}=1\}.
  \label{eq:topological_candidate_set}
\end{equation}
Thus only the $S$--$H$ and $H$--$P$ neighboring pairs can participate in a
soft combination; no direct $S$--$P$ coupling is introduced.

\subsection{Dynamical admissibility of local specialists}
\label{subsec:admissibility_certification}

Routing preference alone is insufficient to determine whether a local model
should contribute to a prediction.  For specialist $j$, initial physical
history $\mathcal{H}$, parameter $\mu$, and rollout horizon $K$, let
\begin{equation}
  m_j(\mathcal{H},\mu,K)\in\{0,1\}
  \label{eq:admissibility_indicator}
\end{equation}
denote a dynamical admissibility indicator.  We set $m_j=1$ when the
specialist is applicable to the parameter--history pair and its autonomous
trajectory over the prescribed horizon remains within the validation-supported
bounded region of its local reduced dynamics.  The concrete boundedness and
finite-state criteria used in the numerical studies are stated with the
evaluation protocol; they are not part of the learned routing score.

Combining adjacency and dynamical admissibility gives the normalized weights
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\pi}_j
  =
  \frac{
    \mathbf{1}_{\{j\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{top}}\}}m_j\pi_j
  }{
    \displaystyle
    \sum_{k\in\mathcal{R}}
    \mathbf{1}_{\{k\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{top}}\}}m_k\pi_k
    +\varepsilon
  }.
  \label{eq:masked_router_weights}
\end{equation}
If only one candidate is admissible, the hierarchy reduces to that
specialist.  If two adjacent candidates are admissible, their independently
evolved predictions are passed to the T2-C mechanism introduced below.  The
hierarchical predictor is defined on the union of parameter--history pairs
for which at least one local specialist is admissible; outside this domain no
ROM prediction is asserted.

\subsection{Top-2 physical-field correction in overlap regions}
\label{subsec:boundary_specific_assembly}

In a regime core, the prediction is obtained directly from the corresponding
specialist,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}
  =
  \begin{cases}
    \widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{S},&\text{steady core},\\
    \widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{H},&\text{Hopf core},\\
    \widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{P},&\text{periodic core}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:core_top1_prediction}
\end{equation}

For an adjacent admissible pair, the E2 probabilities are first normalized on
the pair,
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\pi}_1
  =\frac{\pi_1}{\pi_1+\pi_2},
  \qquad
  \overline{\pi}_2=1-\overline{\pi}_1,
  \qquad
  \ell^{\mathrm{E2}}
  =
  \log\frac{\overline{\pi}_1+\varepsilon}
            {\overline{\pi}_2+\varepsilon}.
  \label{eq:t2c_pair_logit}
\end{equation}
For the $S$--$H$ pair, candidates $1$ and $2$ are the Steady and Hopf
specialists, respectively; for the $H$--$P$ pair they are the Periodic and
Hopf specialists.

The correction gate is conditioned on chart-independent information derived
from the recent physical history.  Let
$\{(\boldsymbol{u}_{n-j},p^{\circ}_{n-j},t_{n-j})\}_{j=0}^{2}$ denote the
current state and two preceding states.  With reference scales
$U_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and $T_{\mathrm{ref}}$, define
\begin{align}
  \widehat E_n^u
  &=\frac{\|\boldsymbol{u}_n\|_{M_u}^2}
          {|\Omega|U_{\mathrm{ref}}^2},
  &
  \widehat E_n^p
  &=\frac{\|p_n^{\circ}\|_{M_p}^2}
          {|\Omega|P_{\mathrm{ref}}^2},
  \label{eq:dimensionless_physical_energies}\\
  \widehat D_n^u
  &=\frac{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}
          {\Delta t_n\sqrt{|\Omega|}U_{\mathrm{ref}}}
    \|\boldsymbol{u}_n-\boldsymbol{u}_{n-1}\|_{M_u},
  &
  \widehat D_n^p
  &=\frac{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}
          {\Delta t_n\sqrt{|\Omega|}P_{\mathrm{ref}}}
    \|p_n^{\circ}-p_{n-1}^{\circ}\|_{M_p},
  \label{eq:dimensionless_physical_change_rates}
\end{align}
where $|\Omega|=\boldsymbol{w}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol{1}$ and
$\Delta t_n=t_n-t_{n-1}$.  We further use the logarithmic energy-growth rate
\begin{equation}
  \widehat g_n
  =
  \frac{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\Delta t_n}
  \log\left(
    \frac{\widehat E_n^u+\varepsilon_E}
         {\widehat E_{n-1}^u+\varepsilon_E}
  \right),
  \label{eq:dimensionless_log_energy_growth}
\end{equation}
and the second-order velocity indicator
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol{A}^{u}_n
  &=
  \frac{2}{\Delta t_n+\Delta t_{n-1}}
  \left[
    \frac{\boldsymbol{u}_n-\boldsymbol{u}_{n-1}}{\Delta t_n}
    -
    \frac{\boldsymbol{u}_{n-1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{n-2}}{\Delta t_{n-1}}
  \right],
  \label{eq:discrete_velocity_acceleration}\\
  \widehat\kappa_n^u
  &=
  \frac{T_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}
       {\sqrt{|\Omega|}U_{\mathrm{ref}}}
  \|\boldsymbol{A}^{u}_n\|_{M_u}.
  \label{eq:dimensionless_velocity_curvature}
\end{align}
These quantities form the chart-independent descriptor
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{s}_n
  =
  [
    \widehat E_n^u,
    \widehat E_n^p,
    \widehat D_n^u,
    \widehat D_n^p,
    \widehat g_n,
    \widehat\kappa_n^u
  ]^{\mathsf T},
  \label{eq:dimensionless_physical_statistics}
\end{equation}
which is standardized componentwise,
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{d}_n
  =
  (\boldsymbol{s}_n-\boldsymbol{\mu}_s)
  \oslash
  (\boldsymbol{\sigma}_s+\varepsilon_s\boldsymbol{1}).
  \label{eq:standardized_physical_descriptors}
\end{equation}
Because $\boldsymbol{d}_n$ is computed directly from physical fields, it is
independent of the POD coordinates of either candidate.

A correction map $c_{\vartheta}$ modifies the pairwise E2 logit according to
\begin{equation}
  \delta
  =
  c_{\vartheta}
  \bigl([\widetilde\mu;\boldsymbol{d}_n]\bigr),
  \qquad
  \alpha
  =
  \operatorname{sigmoid}(\ell^{\mathrm{E2}}+\delta),
  \qquad
  \alpha\in(0,1).
  \label{eq:t2c_convex_gate}
\end{equation}
The scalar $\alpha$ is held fixed over the prediction window.  Both
specialists are initialized by applying their own local encoders to the same
physical history and then evolve independently in their native coordinates;
no coefficient-to-coefficient transformation is required.  At each query
time, only the reconstructed physical fields are combined,
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}(t_q)
  &=
  \alpha\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_1(t_q)
  +(1-\alpha)\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_2(t_q),
  \label{eq:field_fusion}\\
  \widehat p^{\circ}(t_q)
  &=
  \alpha\widehat p^{\circ}_1(t_q)
  +(1-\alpha)\widehat p^{\circ}_2(t_q).
  \label{eq:pressure_field_fusion}
\end{align}
The fused field is not used to update either local specialist.

The correction map is learned from overlap trajectories by minimizing the
relative field error of the convex combination.  If
$\boldsymbol{e}_1^c$ and $\boldsymbol{e}_2^c$ denote the candidate errors for
$c\in\{u,p\}$, then
\begin{equation}
  Q^c(\alpha)
  =
  \alpha^2\|\boldsymbol{e}_1^c\|_{M_c}^{2}
  +(1-\alpha)^2\|\boldsymbol{e}_2^c\|_{M_c}^{2}
  +2\alpha(1-\alpha)
   \langle\boldsymbol{e}_1^c,\boldsymbol{e}_2^c\rangle_{M_c},
  \label{eq:t2c_quadratic_error}
\end{equation}
and the T2-C objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{T2C}}
  =
  \operatorname{mean}_{w,k}
  \left[
    \frac{Q^u(\alpha^{(w)})}
         {\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathrm{true}}\|_{M_u}^{2}+\varepsilon}
    +
    \frac{Q^p(\alpha^{(w)})}
         {\|p^{\circ}_{\mathrm{true}}\|_{M_p}^{2}+\varepsilon}
  \right].
  \label{eq:t2c_training_loss}
\end{equation}
This formulation learns only the relative contribution of two independently
evolved local predictions and does not alter either specialist.

\subsection{Complete hierarchical predictor}
\label{subsec:complete_hierarchical_predictor}

Let $\mathcal{F}_r$ denote the autonomous evolution operator of specialist
$r$ and $\mathcal{D}_r$ its physical reconstruction.  The complete predictor
can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\,\mathrm{phys}}
  =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathcal{D}_{S}\!\circ\mathcal{F}_{S},
    & (\mathcal{H},\mu)\in\Omega_S,\\[1mm]
    \alpha_{SH}\,\mathcal{D}_{S}\!\circ\mathcal{F}_{S}
    +(1-\alpha_{SH})\,\mathcal{D}_{H}\!\circ\mathcal{F}_{H},
    & (\mathcal{H},\mu)\in\Omega_{SH},\\[1mm]
    \mathcal{D}_{H}\!\circ\mathcal{F}_{H},
    & (\mathcal{H},\mu)\in\Omega_H,\\[1mm]
    \alpha_{HP}\,\mathcal{D}_{P}\!\circ\mathcal{F}_{P}
    +(1-\alpha_{HP})\,\mathcal{D}_{H}\!\circ\mathcal{F}_{H},
    & (\mathcal{H},\mu)\in\Omega_{HP},\\[1mm]
    \mathcal{D}_{P}\!\circ\mathcal{F}_{P},
    & (\mathcal{H},\mu)\in\Omega_P.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:complete_ral_moe_predictor}
\end{equation}
The overlap weights $\alpha_{SH}$ and $\alpha_{HP}$ are produced by the
corresponding T2-C gates.  When only one member of an adjacent pair is
dynamically admissible, the overlap branch reduces to that specialist through
\eqref{eq:masked_router_weights}.  Equation~\eqref{eq:complete_ral_moe_predictor}
therefore assembles the local ROMs without introducing a global reduced state:
regime adaptation is performed by selecting or combining complete local
predictions, whereas the internal reduced dynamics remain chart-specific.
