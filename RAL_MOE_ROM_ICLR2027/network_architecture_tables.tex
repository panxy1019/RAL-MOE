for internal routing and parameter counts, and the centered-square specialists
used to construct the routing/fusion candidates. They are not a uniform
architecture shared across benchmarks. Unresolved baseline configurations are
not inferred from these models.

\paragraph{Chart-specific execution.}
Square Steady freezes its velocity experts. The square Hopf candidate uses
direct learned velocity derivatives and direct pressure outputs in its final
rollout wrapper, although projected quantities remain input features. Its
stored pressure gate is not used to assemble the pressure output. In the
adaptive-gate paths, pressure is a gated Poisson base plus a learned residual,
not a convex mixture of two independently predicted candidates.

E2 consumes only the standardized Reynolds number. T2-C consumes that parameter
and eight standardized physical-history descriptors. Its final Linear layer
is initialized to zero, so the initial gate reproduces the selected E2 pair
preference. One scalar fusion weight is fixed for the entire window and shared
by velocity and pressure; no fused field is fed back to either specialist.

All five use cosine learning-rate schedules. Square Steady has a second
parameter group at 0.1 times the listed initial learning rate. Hopf training
uses fluctuation/normal-form losses and validation safeguards in addition to
the common losses. Periodic checkpoints use the validation score combining
RHS, velocity-head, and pressure-head relative errors, with weights
$1,1,0.35$. These objectives and selectors are not uniform across charts.

unsquared component errors. The three gate seeds are 42001, 42002, and 42003.
