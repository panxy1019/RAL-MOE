\subsection{Flow past a centered square cylinder}
\label{subsec:flow_past_centered_square_cylinder}

The flow past a centered square cylinder provides a second test of the
multi-chart construction under a geometry and discretization that differ
from those of the circular-cylinder example.  The parameterized flow is
represented by three overlapping Steady, Hopf and Periodic databases, each
with its own train-only mean, POD basis, pressure closure and native time
advance.  The example therefore tests two distinct questions.  At the local
level, it asks whether the three frozen specialists remain accurate and
finite over their native long-horizon contracts.  At the outer level, it asks
whether the frozen Re-only E2 router and the pair-specific T2-C gates can
exploit complementary predictions in the $S$--$H$ and $H$--$P$ overlaps.
These questions are assessed separately.  Results obtained in different
local POD spaces or at different native horizons are not interpreted as
equal-time, representation-matched rankings.

The high-fidelity model solves the two-dimensional incompressible
Navier--Stokes equations in the channel
$\Omega=[0,31D]\times[0,4D]$.  A square cylinder of side length $D=1$
occupies $[5D,6D]\times[1.5D,2.5D]$, giving a blockage ratio of $0.25$.
The inlet velocity is parabolic,
\begin{equation}
  u_x(y)=4U_m\frac{y(4D-y)}{(4D)^2},
  \qquad u_y=0,
  \qquad U_m=1,
  \label{eq:centered_square_inlet}
\end{equation}
the outlet velocity has zero normal gradient, and no slip is imposed on the
upper and lower channel walls and on the square cylinder.  Pressure is fixed
to zero at the outlet and has zero normal gradient on the remaining
boundaries.  The Reynolds number is $Re=U_mD/\nu$, so that $\nu=1/Re$.

The full-order calculations use OpenFOAM~13 \texttt{icoFoam} with
Crank--Nicolson~0.9 time integration.  The graded finite-volume mesh contains
9,400 cells and is refined around the obstacle and in the near wake.  The CFD
time step is $\delta t_{\mathrm{CFD}}=0.02$, the simulated interval is
$t\in[0,500]$, and one snapshot is retained every 200 CFD steps.  Consecutive
learning snapshots are therefore separated by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta t_{\mathrm{snap}}=4 .
  \label{eq:centered_square_snapshot_dt}
\end{equation}
The combined parameter library contains 120 distinct Reynolds-number cases
over $50\le Re\le150$, with additional sampling in the transition region.

Table~\ref{tab:centered_square_data_contracts} records the three overlapping
specialist domains, their complete-trajectory partitions and their retained
POD dimensions.  Every Reynolds number corresponds to one complete
trajectory.  No trajectory or temporal window is split across training,
validation and held-out roles, and a Reynolds number appearing in two
overlapping specialist domains retains the same split role in both.  The
means, normalization statistics and POD bases are fitted only on the
corresponding training cases.  Velocity and pressure use separate
volume-weighted $L_2$ PODs at the $99.9\%$ cumulative-energy threshold.
Pressure is placed in a common gauge by subtracting its volume-weighted
spatial mean from every snapshot before projection, reconstruction and
evaluation:
\begin{equation}
  p^\circ(\boldsymbol{x},t)
  =
  p(\boldsymbol{x},t)
  -
  \frac{\int_\Omega p(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\Omega}
       {\int_\Omega \mathrm{d}\Omega}.
  \label{eq:centered_square_pressure_gauge}
\end{equation}

All three specialists query the native database at intervals of four physical
time units, but their state-update mechanisms remain local.  The Steady
specialist uses a learned correction in a one-snapshot discrete update, the
Hopf specialist learns snapshot-to-snapshot finite-difference dynamics, and
the Periodic specialist advances its continuous-time model with RK4 using a
maximum internal step of $0.5$ within each database interval.  The native
horizons $K16$, $K24$ and $K48$ therefore span 64, 96 and 192 physical time
units, respectively.  Equal values of $K$ are compared only when the
specialists are run from the same native-source history and evaluated at the
same physical query times.

Checkpoint selection was fixed before held-out field access.  The Steady,
Hopf and Periodic specialists use step~3200, step~7200 and epoch~490,
respectively.  The Re-only E2 router uses step~3300, while the $S$--$H$ and
$H$--$P$ T2-C gates use steps~5500 and 3500.  The split definitions,
pressure gauge, admissible expert pairs, checkpoint-selection rules, error
metrics and final ranking rules were frozen before the sealed evaluation.
No model was retrained or tuned after the held-out fields were loaded.

Prediction quality is evaluated in both physical and reduced coordinates.
The velocity and pressure full-field errors are
\begin{align}
  E_u &=
  \frac{\lVert\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\boldsymbol{u}\rVert_{M_u}}
       {\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert_{M_u}+\varepsilon}\times100\%,
  \label{eq:centered_square_eu}\\
  E_p &=
  \frac{\lVert\widehat{p}^{\,\circ}-p^\circ\rVert_{M_p}}
       {\lVert p^\circ\rVert_{M_p}+\varepsilon}\times100\%,
  \label{eq:centered_square_ep}
\end{align}
and the errors in the local mean-subtracted POD coefficients are
\begin{equation}
  E_a=
  \frac{\lVert\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}-\boldsymbol{a}\rVert_2}
       {\lVert\boldsymbol{a}\rVert_2+\varepsilon}\times100\%,
  \qquad
  E_b=
  \frac{\lVert\widehat{\boldsymbol{b}}-\boldsymbol{b}\rVert_2}
       {\lVert\boldsymbol{b}\rVert_2+\varepsilon}\times100\%.
  \label{eq:centered_square_modal_errors}
\end{equation}
The coefficient errors diagnose fluctuation prediction within one local POD
contract; they are not compared directly across specialists with different
means, bases and ranks.  The outer evaluator additionally reports
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{joint}}=E_u+E_p,
  \label{eq:centered_square_joint}
\end{equation}
but retains $E_u$ and $E_p$ separately so that a pressure-dominated joint
metric cannot conceal the velocity behaviour.  A non-finite rollout, a
registered divergence or a checkpoint/evaluator contract violation is
treated as a fail-closed result: the unsupported test value is not repaired
or replaced by post-test selection.

The Steady specialist completed every held-out $K16$ rollout with finite
fraction one.  Its velocity and pressure errors remained below $1.78\%$ at
all four Reynolds numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:centered_square_specialist_per_re}.  The pressure error was
smallest at $Re=85$, whereas the two cases in the $S$--$H$ overlap,
$Re=95.1$ and 95.3, produced velocity and pressure errors of approximately
$1.7\%$.  The corresponding velocity-coefficient errors were much larger,
ranging from $43.3977\%$ to $56.9322\%$.  This discrepancy indicates that a
mean-containing full field can remain accurate while the lower-energy
fluctuation coordinates are less accurately advanced.  The checkpoint
provides a reproducible finite clean-rollout baseline, but it did not satisfy
all of its preregistered validation deployment conditions and is therefore
not presented as an unconditionally qualified deployment model.

The Hopf specialist was tested over the longer $K48$ horizon.  All 320
held-out windows were finite and none diverged, and the velocity and pressure
field errors stayed below $1\%$ at every test Reynolds number.  The smallest
field errors occurred at $Re=95.3$; the most difficult case was $Re=102$,
with velocity and pressure errors of $0.9669\%$ and $0.8777\%$,
respectively.  Small full-field errors did not imply equally small local
coefficient errors.  In particular, the pressure-coefficient error ranged
from $21.9995\%$ to $91.0229\%$.  The result supports stable and accurate
field reconstruction over the tested horizon while also exposing a
mean-field-dominated error mode that would be hidden by reporting physical
fields alone.

The Periodic specialist completed all held-out $K24$ rollouts without a
non-finite value.  At $Re=100.5$, 102 and 120.689651, the velocity error was
at most $0.6780\%$.  The highest test value, $Re=144.827591$, was the clear
worst case, with velocity and pressure errors of $1.4672\%$ and $1.8023\%$.
Relative to its earlier 240-epoch checkpoint, the long-curriculum model
reduced the $K24$ velocity and pressure errors by $92.79\%$ and $92.95\%$,
respectively.  This comparison attributes the improvement to adequate
multi-step curriculum training rather than to a test-time model choice.

The outer hierarchy is evaluated only under a common native-source contract.
The frozen E2 model first gives a trajectory/query-level Top-1 decision from
$Re$.  A T2-C gate is enabled only when both adjacent specialists have passed
the same native-source preflight.  For each window, the gate reads $Re$ and
descriptors of the initial three-frame history and produces one convex weight,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{\mathrm{T2C}}
  =
  \alpha_1\widehat{\boldsymbol{q}}_1
  +(1-\alpha_1)\widehat{\boldsymbol{q}}_2,
  \qquad 0\le\alpha_1\le1 .
  \label{eq:centered_square_t2c}
\end{equation}
The weight is fixed throughout the $K24$ window.  Both specialists advance
independently from the same three-frame physical history, the combination is
formed only on their aligned physical outputs, and no fused state is fed back
to either rollout.  The $S$--$H$ and $H$--$P$ development preflights contained
72 and 48 windows, respectively; both candidates were finite on every window
and neither pair produced a divergence.  The selected validation joint means
were $1.0639\%$ for $S$--$H$ and $0.7906\%$ for $H$--$P$.

Table~\ref{tab:centered_square_fusion_aggregate} compares the two individual
candidates, E2 Top-1, two fixed router-derived blends, T2-C and the
per-window convex oracle under the sealed held-out $K24$ evaluator.  Every
method in one boundary block uses the same initial histories, query times,
grid, area weights and pressure gauge.  These values can therefore be
compared directly.

At the $S$--$H$ boundary, E2 Top-1 selected the Hopf candidate and obtained a
joint error of $1.7298\%$.  T2-C reduced this value to $1.1958\%$, a relative
decrease of $30.87\%$, and reduced the worst-window joint error from
$9.1233\%$ to $4.8304\%$.  At the $H$--$P$ boundary, Periodic was the best
single candidate with a joint error of $1.2079\%$, whereas the hard E2
decisions produced $1.8473\%$ after aggregation over the two test parameters.
T2-C reduced the joint error to $0.8465\%$, corresponding to decreases of

T2-C mean lay close to the per-window convex oracle, indicating that the
lightweight gate recovered most of the output-level complementarity available
under the frozen candidate pair.

The per-parameter results in
Table~\ref{tab:centered_square_fusion_per_re} clarify where the aggregate
improvements arose.  At both $S$--$H$ test values, the mean Steady weight was
approximately $0.097$, so the combined prediction remained Hopf-dominated.
The window-wise Steady weight nevertheless ranged from nearly zero to about
$0.77$.  T2-C reduced the joint error by $31.00\%$ at $Re=95.1$ and by
$30.74\%$ at $Re=95.3$.  The $S$--$H$ improvement was therefore present at
both held-out parameters rather than being produced by one favourable case.

The $H$--$P$ gate made a stronger correction to the hard router.  At
$Re=100.5$, E2 selected Hopf, whereas T2-C assigned a mean weight of $0.587$
to Periodic and reduced the joint error from $2.6485\%$ to $0.8571\%$, a
$67.64\%$ decrease.  At $Re=102$, E2 selected Periodic, while T2-C retained a
mean Hopf contribution of $0.291$ and reduced the joint error by a further
$20.09\%$.  The $H$--$P$ result consequently did not arise from mechanical
collapse to one candidate.  The weights varied with the parameter and the
initial history, and the combination improved both test parameters without
introducing a non-finite or divergent window.

The results support several deliberately limited conclusions.  First, all
three specialists produced finite, non-divergent held-out rollouts under
their own native contracts, but low full-field errors did not guarantee low
fluctuation-coordinate errors.  This distinction was most visible in the
Steady velocity coefficients and the Hopf pressure coefficients.  Second,
the outer improvement came from output-level complementarity between
independently advanced adjacent specialists; it did not require a common
latent state or feed a blended state back into either local model.  Third,
T2-C improved E2 Top-1 at all four held-out boundary parameters and reduced
the aggregate worst-window error without sacrificing finite execution.

The frozen assets do not contain Vanilla-FNN-MoE, DataOnly-MoE and Global MoE
results under the same CenteredSquare split, pressure gauge, native horizon
and sealed evaluator.  Numbers from a different contract are not inserted to
create an apparent ablation table.  The local attribution in this example is
therefore restricted to the three frozen specialist reports and to the
Periodic long-curriculum comparison with its own 240-epoch predecessor.  The
outer attribution is restricted to the matched candidates, E2 Top-1, fixed
and probability blends, T2-C and the oracle listed in
Table~\ref{tab:centered_square_fusion_aggregate}.

Soft combination requires both candidate rollouts and therefore costs
approximately two native specialist advances plus a negligible gate
evaluation, whereas E2 Top-1 advances only one specialist.  The reported
single-GPU training times were 3.351~h for the Hopf specialist and 12.83~h
for the Periodic specialist; the Steady report did not provide a comparable
wall-clock value.  Once the frozen dual-rollout caches had been constructed,
optimization of E2, the $S$--$H$ gate and the $H$--$P$ gate took approximately
14.6, 17.7 and 18.5~s, respectively.  These values indicate scale only:
the specialist budgets, batches and time-advance implementations were not
matched for an efficiency ranking.

Taken together, the centered-square results support independent local
representations and native dynamical contracts for the three overlapping
flow regimes.  The Steady checkpoint supplied a finite clean-rollout baseline
but retained large velocity-coefficient errors and did not satisfy every

four held-out boundary parameters, the weights varied with both parameter

non-divergent.  The evidence therefore supports a contract-aware local

representation-matched ranking against unavailable global or architecture
