\begin{abstract}
Reduced-order models (ROMs) become difficult to use over broad parameter ranges when parameter variation changes the qualitative dynamics of the system. A single global reduced space and a single reduced dynamical model must then accommodate regimes with different spatial structures and temporal behavior, which can compromise autonomous long-horizon prediction. To address these challenges, we introduce RAL-MoE-ROM, a two-level hierarchical mixture-of-experts ROM that adapts both the reduced representation and the reduced dynamics. The parameter domain is covered by overlapping regime-local reduced spaces with independent coordinates. Within each such space, a physics--data specialist combines projected ROM dynamics with a sparsely routed set of structured expert corrections for unresolved dynamics. At the second level, a parameter-only router selects either one complete local specialist or an admissible adjacent pair of specialists. Since the local reduced coordinates are independent and not componentwise comparable, specialists are advanced
separately rather than blended in coefficient space. When two specialists are selected, their reconstructed fields are combined only in the common physical space by a learned, history-conditioned convex correction (T2-C). Across three parameterized incompressible-flow benchmarks spanning steady, Hopf-onset, and periodic regimes, the proposed specialists achieve accurate autonomous rollouts, and the hierarchical fusion improves prediction in the evaluated transition regions. Ablations support the roles of the projected
backbone, structured expert correction, regime localization, and
history-conditioned fusion. These results demonstrate a practical route to reduced modeling across changing dynamical regimes without forcing all
local models into a shared reduced coordinate system.
\end{abstract}

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}
The main text reports the benchmark roles, evaluation horizons, baselines, and metrics. Appendices~A--E document the local-coordinate construction, projected operators, specialist architecture, routing and fusion procedure, and implementation details. Appendices~F--H provide benchmark-specific results.
Appendix~\ref{app:internal_routing} reports post-hoc internal-routing statistics
from fixed validation-selected Hopf and Periodic specialists on held-out
rollout windows; these diagnostics are not used for model selection.
Appendix~\ref{app:square} additionally reports an architecture-matched descriptor
ablation and three-seed variability of the centered-square T2-C gate, with the
underlying specialists and E2 router held fixed.
